# Before the Heavens Tear

## Prologue: Three in the Morning

Three in the morning. The house is still. The refrigerator hums. You are awake.

Some of us cannot sleep. We are older now, and once we wake, sleep will not come back. Some of us must not sleep. A child has a fever, and her forehead burns against your palm. Some of us will not sleep. The phone lights our face blue, and we scroll until we ache.

Today the church year begins. Advent means "coming." We get ready for the Lord who came once, at Christmas, and we wait for the Lord who will come again in glory. We live between the two.

Tonight Jesus speaks to people who are awake. He says, "Stay awake." But being awake and keeping watch are not the same thing.

## Act One: Praying on Scorched Ground

Twenty-five centuries ago, a company of exiles came home from Babylon to Jerusalem. The temple was a heap of blackened stone. When the wind moved, ash still lifted from it. "Our holy and beautiful house, where our fathers praised you, has been burned by fire" (Isa 64:11).

That ruin was no accident of history. It was judgment.

Their fathers had broken covenant with God. A covenant is a bond like a marriage. God had said, "I will be your God, and you shall be my people." But the kings led the way to idols. King Manasseh burned his own son as an offering, and "shed very much innocent blood, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another" (2 Kgs 21:6, 16; 24:3-4). Worst of all, much of it was done in God's name. Jeremiah stood at the temple gate and cried out: You steal, you murder, you commit adultery, you chase other gods, and then you walk into this house and say, "This is the temple of the LORD," and think you are safe. "Has this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes?" (Jer 7:4-11)

The leaders went wrong. The people followed. Ruin fell on them all. When the city fell, most of those who starved, who died by the sword, who were marched off in chains, were ordinary households that had followed their leaders. Their children wept in the rubble: "Our fathers sinned, and are no more; and we bear their iniquities" (Lam 5:7).

On that ground, ground under judgment they had earned, they cried out: "Oh that you would rend the heavens and come down!" (Isa 64:1)

This is not the protest of the innocent. It is the cry of sinners. They do not ask, "Why us?" They say, "Behold, you were angry, and we sinned; in our sins we have been a long time, and shall we be saved?" (Isa 64:5)

## Act Two: We Are the Clay

The Bible's word for sin first meant an arrow that misses the mark. But this is no light miss. An arrow that misses the target strikes someone else. When people break covenant with God, cities burn, temples fall, and children are carried away.

So they confess: "All our righteous deeds are like a polluted garment" (Isa 64:6). Even our acts of piety, even the things we did in God's name, are soiled. "We all fade like a leaf, and our iniquities, like the wind, take us away." Then: "You have hidden your face from us, and have made us melt in the hand of our iniquities" (Isa 64:7). They feel themselves melting down in their own sin.

The lament's last line is left out of today's reading: "Will you keep silent, and afflict us so terribly?" (Isa 64:12)

Yet at the lowest point the prayer turns. "But now, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand" (Isa 64:8). They have no righteousness left to show. They have one name left: clay. When clay cracks, only the potter can make it again. Picture his hands wetting the broken lump, working it soft, pressing it back onto the wheel.

Psalm 80 prays from the ruins of the northern kingdom: "O LORD God of hosts, how long will you be angry with your people's prayers? You have fed them with the bread of tears" (Ps 80:4-5). Its refrain comes three times, and each time the name of God grows fuller: "O God," then "O God of hosts," then "O LORD God of hosts. Restore us, O LORD God of hosts! Let your face shine, that we may be saved!" (Ps 80:3, 7, 19)

## Act Three: The Temple Falls Again

Five centuries later the temple stood again, its stones gleaming white. A disciple said, "Look, Teacher, what wonderful stones!" Jesus answered that not one stone would be left upon another (Mark 13:1-2).

Why? A few days earlier Jesus had overturned the money changers' tables in that very temple, quoting Jeremiah's own words: "But you have made it a den of robbers" (Mark 11:17). The old sin had come back. Men did their own business in God's name. The chief priests were already plotting to kill Jesus (Mark 14:1). Forty years later, in AD 70, Roman legions burned that temple. Mark's first readers read this chapter with the smoke still in the air.

Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives, facing the temple, and says: the sun will be darkened, the stars will fall, "and the powers in the heavens will be shaken" (Mark 13:24-25). People once looked at kings the way they looked at stars, fixed and forever. Our stars are nations, markets, and every power that uses God's name to devour people. Jesus says they will all fall. Then the Son of Man will come on the clouds and gather his own from the four winds, home (Mark 13:26-27).

## Act Four: The Four Watches

The disciples ask, "When?" Jesus says, "No one knows" (Mark 13:32). Instead he tells a story. A master leaves on a journey. He puts his servants in charge, "each with his work, and commands the doorkeeper to stay awake" (Mark 13:34). Keeping watch does not mean staring at the sky. It means keeping the work the master put in your hands. Here is our third key word: Keep What He Entrusted.

The master may come "in the evening, or at midnight, or when the rooster crows, or in the morning" (Mark 13:35). Two days later those four hours arrive, one by one. Evening: the Last Supper. Midnight: Gethsemane. Rooster crow: Peter denies him three times. Morning: Jesus is handed over to Pilate.

In Gethsemane Jesus falls to the ground and asks the Father, "Remove this cup from me" (Mark 14:36). The prophets knew that cup. It was "the cup of his wrath" (Isa 51:17), the cup of judgment. "And he came and found them sleeping" (Mark 14:37). That night, only he stayed awake.

The next day at noon "there was darkness over the whole land" (Mark 15:33). The sun was darkened, just as chapter 13 said. Jesus cried, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Mark 15:34) He walked onto the ground of judgment, into the very center of the scorched earth. When he breathed his last, "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Mark 15:38).

Isaiah begged God to tear the heavens and come down. God did tear them open. But the place he came down to was the heart of judgment. He did not pardon us from a distance. He drank the cup himself.

Here is our fourth key word: He Keeps Watch for Me. Before the rooster crowed, he told Peter, "I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail" (Luke 22:32). We fall asleep. We stumble. We do wrong in God's name. "He who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep" (Ps 121:4).

## Act Five: God Is Faithful

Corinth was a city that measured everyone. The church there split into camps, each comparing its spiritual gifts (1 Cor 1:10-12). Yet Paul begins with thanks to God: "You are not lacking in any gift, as you wait for the revealing of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 1:7). He goes on: "who will sustain you to the end, guiltless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ. God is faithful" (1 Cor 1:8-9).

Guiltless. Not because we did no wrong, but because the Lord who stood at the center of judgment stands there for us.

So this week, keep watch on the road you already walk. When you help a parent to the bathroom in the night, say, "Lord, you gave me this." If you wake at three and cannot sleep, make that watch an hour of prayer, and name your children and grandchildren one by one. If you are young and still scrolling, turn the phone face down and say one line: "Tear the heavens and come down."

Keep watch for the church, too. When leaders go wrong, ordinary people still suffer with them. It happens in our day. Pray for those who lead. And find the courage to say, "This is not the heart of God." What one person cannot do, the church does together as the body of Christ.

## Epilogue: Before the Heavens Tear

Take these four key words home as four steps for the week:

Tear the Heavens, Come Down. Cry out.
We Are the Clay. Yield.
Keep What He Entrusted. Do your part.
He Keeps Watch for Me. Rest.

The master will come home. Tonight, keep what he entrusted to you. Then sleep in peace, because he is awake. Amen.
