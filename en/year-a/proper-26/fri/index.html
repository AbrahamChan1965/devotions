Friday Instructio: Laying Down Self, Laboring Night and Day

This week | RCL Proper 26 (November 1, 2026)
Today | Friday, October 30, 2026 · moving toward the Sunday of November 1

TODAY'S READINGS

Epistle1 Thessalonians 2:9–13 讀經CUV ReadWEB

"For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: we worked night and day, that we might not be a burden to any of you, while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God." (1 Thess 2:9)

This week the Pharisees lay heavy burdens on shoulders while their own fingers rest; Paul lays the burden on his own shoulders and burdens no one. One seizes the deed; one lays down self. Same week, two kinds of people, face to face.

First, the laying down. Paul had the right — he says so plainly in 1 Corinthians 9: those who preach the gospel should live from the gospel; the Lord commanded it. "Nevertheless, we have not made use of this right, but we endure anything rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ" (1 Cor 9:12). The right was real; the laying-down was real. The deed-seizer's essence is clutching everything deserved; Paul's essence was releasing everything obtainable. He made tents — worked by day, preached by night. This is Philippians 2 as biography: "though he was in the form of God... he emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant" (Phil 2:6–7). Kenosis — self-emptying. Paul's night-and-day labor is Christ's self-emptying in work clothes.

Now the Pharisees. Their problem is precisely the inability to lay down: the best seats unrelinquished, the greetings unrelinquished, the title "rabbi" unrelinquished, the phylactery-width unrelinquished. Unable to lay down, they could only transfer the weight to others. A person who can't lay down self will always shift the load onto someone else — that's spiritual physics. This week's two religions reduce to a formula: deed-seizers transfer burdens; self-layers-down carry them.

Then, the labor's direction: "that we might not be a burden to any of you, while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God." Note the object of his nights and days: not his own estate, but "you" — the gospel for them. His nights had a direction. Many labor night and day — for mortgages, promotions, profiles. But Paul's nights were for "you": for those who hadn't crossed the river yet. That's the difference: same late hours; some become owners, some become servants.

The peak is verse 13: "When you received the word of God, which you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers" (1 Thess 2:13). "At work" — energeō: energy, activated. God's word isn't information; it's energy — once inside, it moves on its own. The Pharisees' phylacteries held God's word too — Deuteronomy 6, genuine Scripture. Same word: in the Pharisees it wouldn't run; in the Thessalonians it ran. The difference isn't in the word; it's in the person. One wears the word as jewelry (worn to be seen); the other receives it as seed (seed must fall and die). "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone" (John 12:24). Jewelry belongs to owners; seed belongs to tenants — and Paul was the man who sowed himself like seed.

Chrysostom said Paul sets the pattern: ministry isn't a matter of the mouth but of life — "he gave them his own soul" (cf. 2:8). The mouth gives the message; the life gives the self. The Pharisees gave mouth without life; Paul gave even his life. That's why "imitate us" (1:6) doesn't blush on his lips — his hands testified for his mouth.

There's a final precision: Paul says "you are witnesses, and God also, how holy and righteous and blameless was our conduct" (v. 10). He can invoke witnesses because his nights were public — tents made in the marketplace, sermons preached in the open. Deed-seizers operate in back rooms (Jezebel's letters, sealed); servants operate in daylight. If your labor can't bear witnesses, check whether it's labor or seizure.

Tomorrow is Saturday, our dialogue with our elder brothers; Sunday's sermon returns to Joshua 3. What was Paul laboring night and day for? For the day he'd see those who received the word — the word at work in their hearts — in white robes before the Lamb. His labor's destination was the white-robed multitude. Ask today: what are your nights and days for? Your own deed, or someone else's white robe?

Let's pray: Lord who emptied Himself — who, being in the form of God, took the form of a servant. We confess we clutch everything deserved: deserved respect, deserved returns, deserved best seats; all we can't lay down is ourselves. Forgive us. Give us Paul's heart — laying down self, laboring night and day for You; give us Paul's hands — carrying burdens, burdening no one; give us the Thessalonians' ears — hearing Your word and recognizing Your voice. Let Your word work in our hearts until we too wear white. Amen.
