Two Tables

Catching yesterday's question. Yesterday asked: whose burden can I carry a little today? Today the two tables answer in the same shape with different hands: Abraham running to serve his guests, the Shunammite preparing a room — carrying takes the form of welcome.

Time Coordinate. For the synagogue, today is the 20th of Cheshvan, 5787, and the reading is Vayera; for the Church, today is the eve of Sunday at the end of Ordinary Time. Two tables are set on the same day, each eating its own, each speaking its own.

The synagogue's table. This week's Parashat is Vayera — "He appeared." The LORD appears to Abraham at Mamre's oaks, "in the heat of the day." Abraham sits (Genesis 18:1); seeing three men standing (18:2), he runs to meet them, bows to the ground, asks them to wash their feet (18:4), hurries into the tent (18:6), runs to the herd (18:7), and at last stands beside them while they eat (18:8). Abraham had everything, yet never thought of himself as the owner of Canaan's good land — when he came to bury Sarah, he bought the cave of Machpelah at full price (Genesis 23); he did not seize it.

Sarah overhears "about this time next year... you shall have a son" and laughs. God asks: "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" The Haftarah (2 Kings 4) echoes: the Shunammite, a great woman (4:8), prepares a small upper room — bed, table, chair, lamp (4:10) — and welcomes Elisha. She says, "I dwell among my own people" (4:13): she wants no one to speak to the king for her. Later her son dies; she embraces the prophet's feet (4:27); he puts his mouth upon the boy's mouth, his eyes upon his eyes, his hands upon his hands (4:34), and walks back and forth in the house (4:35), and the child lives.

The rabbis say: welcoming guests is greater than receiving the Shekhinah (b. Shabbat 127a). What Abraham did with his own hands for the messengers, the Holy One did with His own hands for Israel in the wilderness (b. Bava Metzia 86b).

The synagogue reads on to the heaviest passage: Genesis 22, the Akedah (the Binding). God asks Abraham for Isaac. Isaac asks, "Where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" Abraham answers, "God will provide for himself the lamb" (Genesis 22:7–8). In the end, a ram instead of Isaac — Isaac lives.

The Church's table. This week's lections set another table. Joshua 3: priests carry the ark on their shoulders, feet touch the water, and they stand firm on dry ground in the middle of the river until all the people have crossed (Joshua 3:17). Matthew 23: men sit on Moses' seat, love the first seat and the title, tie burdens on others' shoulders, and will not move them with a finger — dwelling long in the land of grace, they came to think themselves owners of God's estate. 1 Thessalonians 2: Paul does the reverse, working night and day so as not to burden anyone. Psalm 107: the redeemed open their mouths — "Let the redeemed of the LORD say so."

The Church's table also speaks of a Son. The estate-owner's Son in Jesus' parable was led up a hill, carrying wood, asking why — and no ram came to die in his place. The Church believes: He Himself is the Lamb; the tenants killed the estate-owner's Son, and the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' very sin.

The divergence, and we stop here. The synagogue says: the Holy One who feeds His people with His own hands is Himself our hope; we still await redemption. The Church says: we believe the same, and we believe the One He sent has come, named Jesus. Two tables face to face, two voices whole and present. The divergence falls precisely here: who is the Redeemer, and has He come? We stop here. No debate, no closure. Our safety comes from atonement, not from winning the argument, so we can listen quietly.

Today's Practice (about twenty minutes). 1. Read Genesis 18:1–8, and note one word you cannot take for yourself. (Ten minutes.) 2. Write that word down, and leave the space after it blank. (Five minutes.) 3. Sit quietly, with both voices present. (Five minutes.)

Today I heard a word I cannot take for myself: ____

Carry-away question: Tomorrow, from which seat will I rise?

Closing Prayer: Creator of heaven and earth, you chose Israel; you are not Israel's possession. Holy One of Israel, you appeared at Mamre's oaks, and stood in the middle of the Jordan. We confess: having dwelt long here, we think ourselves owners of God's estate, sitting to collect rent, refusing to run and serve. Teach us to run as Abraham ran toward his guests; teach us to remember that we are tenants bought back, not owners. Amen.
