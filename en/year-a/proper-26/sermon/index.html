Sunday Sermon: The Tenants' Ransom

SUNDAY SERMON · November 1, 2026
SERMON NAIL
Dwelling long in the land of grace, they came to think they owned it. The tenants killed the estate-owner's Son — yet the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' very sin.
This Sunday | RCL Proper 26 (November 1, 2026)
Readings | Joshua 3:7–17 (main); Matthew 23:1–12 (application); 1 Thessalonians 2:9–13
Preaching time | approx. 12 minutes

The nail for the whole sermon, driven first: dwelling long in the land of grace, they came to think they owned it. The tenants killed the estate-owner's Son — yet the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' very sin.

## 1. Three generations

Nail: the first generation died in the wilderness; the second crossed the river; the third thinks it owns the place — the third generation of grace is the most dangerous.

Read Joshua 3 with three-generations eyes. Generation one: the exodus generation — all but Joshua and Caleb fell in the desert. They saw the Red Sea parted yet didn't trust God for Canaan. Seeing miracles isn't trusting God.

Generation two: today's text. Joshua — his name is Jesus (Yeshua, "the LORD saves") — leads the new generation to put their feet in the flooded Jordan. Four hundred years from the patriarchs' promise to this day. God takes the vineyard back and hands it over.

Generation three: the one that dwelt long in the land of grace and came to think it owned it. Their fathers killed the prophets; they kept killing. God sent His Son to collect the fruit; they killed the Son. Brothers and sisters — which generation are we? We live in generation three: raised on the gospel, grown up in church, grace heard until it's background noise. This sermon is for us.

## 2. The crossing

Nail: stopping the water is God's job; extending the foot is yours.

"When the feet of the priests bearing the ark were dipped in the brink of the water... the waters stood, rising up in a heap" (Josh 3:15–16). Note the order: feet first, waters second. God doesn't pave the road and then invite; He waits for wet feet.

Note the formation too: the ark goes first. God's presence walks into the flood ahead of us. You never cross alone; the One who commands the river is already ankle-deep ahead of you. And God's way of exalting Joshua was to get his feet wet before everyone — "Today I will begin to exalt you in the sight of all Israel" (Josh 3:7). The road to exaltation runs through river water; God doesn't exalt those who won't get wet.

## 3. The tenants' indictment

Nail: they preach, but do not practice; all their deeds are done to be seen.

Here we turn to application — to Matthew 23. Jesus indicts not outsiders but the vineyard's third generation: "They preach, but do not practice. They do all their deeds to be seen by others" (Matt 23:3, 5). Phylacteries widened, fringes lengthened, loving best seats at feasts, greetings in marketplaces, being called rabbi.

This is the landlord mind fully grown: tying up heavy burdens for others' shoulders while one's own finger rests (Matt 23:4). Rules for others, exemptions for self. Brothers and sisters, the indictment faces us today: have we turned church into property? Ministry into facade? Piety into jewelry? The third commandment says, "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain" — God's name on the lips, what God hates in the hands: that is taking it in vain.

His rebuke escalates to seven woes. But don't skip 23:37: after the woes, He weeps — "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets." The Judge's tears. He didn't come to vent; He came heartbroken.

## 4. The killing that is grace

Nail: the tenants killed the Son; the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' sin.

Remember the date: Holy Week. Jesus speaks these words days before the Last Supper; the next day He will be killed by these tenants. He knows. He speaks anyway.

And that killing is grace arriving. The tenants reasoned, "This is the heir. Come, let us kill him and have his inheritance" (Matt 21:38) — killing the Son as property strategy: remove the heir, seize the estate. They succeeded. But God's answer: the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' very sin. The Owner didn't send an army to repossess the vineyard; He sent His Son. The Son didn't come with a sword; He came with a cross. No other religion on earth talks like this: the killed becomes the killers' ransom.

## 5. Application: white robes before the King returns

Nail: before the King returns, tenants can still wear white — white robes aren't prizes; they're washed.

This is the church year's kairos: late Ordinary Time, three weeks to Advent — the King is coming. The tenants' time is short. But the good news: it hasn't run out.

Before the crossing, Joshua commanded: "Consecrate yourselves, for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you" (Josh 3:5). Consecration — that is the application. Generation three's way out isn't leaving the vineyard; it's consecration: confessing we are tenants, not owners; ransomed, not deserving.

And white robes are what consecrated people wear. Revelation 7's multitude "washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Rev 7:14) — white robes aren't prizes; they're washed. "They shall hunger no more, neither thirst anymore... and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (Rev 7:16–17). To those growing numb while waiting for the King: choose. Keep playing landlord — or consecrate yourself now, and wear white?

## Conclusion

Back to the opening nail: dwelling long in the land of grace, they came to think they owned it. The tenants killed the estate-owner's Son — yet the Son's death became the ransom for the tenants' very sin.

Generation one died in the wilderness. Generation two crossed. Generation three — us — stands at the choice. The Owner's Son has been killed; the ransom is paid. One act remains: consecrate, cross, wear white.

## Memory exercise

This week's text: Joshua 3:5 — "Consecrate yourselves, for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you."

Four-step method: (1) Read aloud three times, slowly, hearing your own voice. (2) Cover the second half, recite the first; then reverse. (3) Recite backwards from the last word — backward recitation locks memory. (4) Recite again tomorrow morning; repeat daily for seven days.

Catchphrase: consecrate, then wonders. First consecration, then God's wonders. Tenants who consecrate are the ones who get to wear white.

Let's pray: O Owner who sent His Son into the vineyard — we are those tenants. We confess: dwelling long in Your grace, we thought we owned it; Your name on our lips, what You hate in our hands. Forgive our vain use of Your name. And thank You — thank You that after we killed Your Son, You sent no army, repossessed nothing, but let His blood become the ransom for this very sin of ours. Consecrate us; bring us across. Before the King returns, clothe us in washed white. Amen.
