Sunday Sermon: Before the King Returns

SUNDAY SERMON · October 25, 2026
THE NAIL
Two pegs hold everything; one King is coming.
Text: Matthew 22:34–46 (RCL Proper 25)
Date: Sunday, October 25, 2026
Liturgical moment: Late Ordinary Time, four weeks before Advent
Memory text this week: Matthew 22:37–40

Grace and peace to you. Before we open the text, memorize one sentence — the nail of today's sermon. If you remember one thing from today, remember this:

Two pegs hold everything; one King is coming.

Introduction: Where Are We in the Calendar?

We are standing at the tail end of Ordinary Time. In four weeks — November 29 — Advent begins, and the church will light the first candle. The church year doesn't end with a New Year's countdown; it ends with a countdown to the coming King.

Look at the Matthew readings of the past five weeks: September 20, the workers in the vineyard (20:1–16). September 27, the two sons (21:23–32). October 4, the wicked tenants (21:33–46). October 11, the wedding banquet (22:1–14). October 18, Caesar's coin (22:15–22). Today, the greatest commandment (22:34–46). All from Matthew 20–22 — Jesus on the road to Jerusalem, entering the city, confronting the religious leaders in the temple in His final week. Every week, the cross draws one day nearer. Today's text is among the last public words of the King, spoken in the temple days before the cross. Read it, and listen for the countdown.

1. The Last Week's Summary

[Nail] Those about to leave say what matters most.

The Pharisees send a lawyer to "test" Jesus — the word means to trap. Jewish tradition counted 613 commandments; name one "greatest" and you offend the rest; fail to answer and the Nazarene rabbi is finished.

Jesus answers in two sentences: "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind" (Deut 6:5). "And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself" (Lev 19:18). "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets" (v. 40). "Hang" — kremaō. The whole Law is a garment hanging on two pegs: love God, love your neighbor.

When a person is about to leave, they say what matters most. In His final week, Jesus distilled everything into two sentences. This isn't an introductory course; it's a last-will-and-testament level teaching. Augustine's verdict stands: the destination of all Scripture is these two loves (On Christian Doctrine).

Those about to leave say what matters most. Jesus left His most important words with us. Today, He places these two sentences in your hands.

2. Who Is the Christ? The Coming King

[Nail] David's Lord is the coming King.

Jesus turns the question around: "What do you think of the Messiah? Whose son is he?" "The son of David." "How is it then that David by the Spirit calls him Lord?" (vv. 42–45, citing Ps 110:1). Silence. "No one dared to ask him any more questions" (v. 46).

Read this inside the calendar: in four weeks comes Advent — the church remembers His first coming and waits for His second, the Parousia. The "Lord" David spoke of is the King Advent waits for. Confess Him as Lord today, and you won't be ashamed when you see Him (1 John 2:28).

David's Lord is the coming King. The lectionary places this text at year's end to force the question before we welcome the King: who is He? Is He your Lord?

3. Paul: Shepherding in the Shadow of the Return

[Nail] Gentleness is eschatology with skin on.

1 Thessalonians 2:1–8 — Paul "gentle among you, like a nurse tenderly caring for her own children," ready to share "not only the gospel of God but also our own selves." But don't read this as generic niceness. Read its setting.

1 Thessalonians is one of the New Testament's great eschatological letters. Paul planted that church in three Sabbaths (Acts 17:2) — and he is clear it wasn't his eloquence: it was "the power of God and the work of the Spirit" (1 Thess 1:5). His whole argument unfolds under the coming of the King: two weeks from now the lectionary gives us 4:13–18 — the Lord's descent, the resurrection of the dead; the week after, 5:1–11 — the Day of the Lord coming like a thief in the night.

So chapter 2's gentleness is eschatological gentleness: precisely because the King is returning, Paul treats people with eternal eyes; precisely because days are numbered, he gives his very self. His fatherly-motherly heart isn't a personality trait — it's eschatology with skin on. It echoes our devotionals all week: Moses counting his days (Ps 90); Paul counting souls before the Parousia.

Gentleness is eschatology with skin on. Those who know the King is returning treat people differently.

4. The Day of the Lord: Solemn

[Nail] On that Day, only love abides.

In the Old Testament, "the Day of the LORD" was never a cozy phrase. Joel calls it "great and terrible" (Joel 2:31). Amos warns, "Woe to you who desire the day of the LORD!" (Amos 5:18). And two weeks from now — November 15 — the lectionary gives us Zephaniah 1:14–18, the Day of judgment. The calendar is walking us straight toward it.

So "the hinge of love" isn't a devotional nicety; it's the final exam. On that Day, God won't grade your theology paper — He'll look at which two pegs your life was hanging on. Paul writes, "Faith, hope, and love abide" (1 Cor 13:13) — on the Day of the Lord, what abides is love.

On that Day, only love abides. Hang on the right pegs today, and you'll stand on that Day.

Memory Exercise This Week

Memory text: Matthew 22:37–40. A four-beat method — do it with your family:

- Beat 1 (Mon): "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind." - Beat 2 (Tue): "And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself." - Beat 3 (Wed): "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets." - Beat 4 (Thu): recite all three together; write them out on Friday; recite to your family on Saturday; we'll recite together on Sunday.

Six words to carry: Love God. Love neighbor. The hinge. Two pegs hold everything.

Conclusion: Before the King Returns

In four weeks, Advent's first candle will be lit, and the church will sing, "Come, Lord Jesus." Today, in the temple, He asks, "Who do you say the Messiah is?" In four weeks, we'll spend the whole season answering.

Two concrete responses. First: go home today, find the hardest person for you to love, and do one small, secret kindness for them. Store up love before the King returns. Second: every morning ask, "Today, who gets my 'all my heart, soul, and mind'?" Check it at night.

Remember today's nail: Two pegs hold everything; one King is coming.

Let's pray: Lord Jesus, David called You Lord, and so do we. Days before the cross, You left us the hinge of love; weeks before Advent, You ask us who You are. Forgive us for hanging our lives on the wrong pegs. Before the King returns, teach us to count our days — and to spend them on the only two things that hold everything up: loving You, and loving our neighbors. Amen.
