Beginning and End

I. Scripture Anchor: "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you." (Genesis 12:1) — set beside "I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there." (Deuteronomy 34:4)

Today is the Sabbath. Right now, in synagogues, our elder brothers in the Old Testament are chanting this week's portion: *Lech-Lecha* — "Go forth." And the passage we have been reading all week is the end of Moses (Deuteronomy 34). Theirs is a beginning; ours is an ending. Theirs is Abraham, seventy-five, striking his tents; ours is Moses, one hundred and twenty, falling at the doorway of the promise. Today, from the Christian side, we draw near to our elder brothers — listening to what they read, and letting our Scriptures converse with theirs.

— On their side: a single word, "Go."

*Lech-Lecha* opens with God speaking to Abram: "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you" (Genesis 12:1). No map. No timetable. Only a command and a promise. Jewish tradition regards this as the archetype of faith — faith does not wait to see before it walks; it walks, and then it sees.

The Haftarah, Isaiah 40:27–41:16, is a song for the exhausted. Israel on the eve of exile stood, like Abraham, at the doorway of departure. The prophet hands them their ancestor's story: "Those who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles" (Isaiah 40:31).

— On our side: a single word, "Stop."

We, this week, have been reading "stop." "I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there" (Deuteronomy 34:4). To see, and not to enter — among the quietest tragedies in Scripture.

— The conversation: between "Go" and "Stop."

And yet both sides tell one story: a human life is movement inside God's promise. Abraham moved his body; Moses moved a people. Abraham died "not having received the things promised" (Hebrews 11:13); Moses never walked into Canaan. Faith was never arrival. It was always the road.

Even the image of flight is in dialogue. We read Moses: "we fly away" (Psalm 90:10) — flight as the brevity of life. Our brothers read "mount up with wings like eagles" — flight as the renewal of faith. One motion, and the answer hides in one word: waiting. Those who number their days wait for God to establish the work of their hands; those who wait for the LORD renew their strength.

From the Christian side, we perceive one further line — and we say honestly that our brothers may not walk it with us: Abraham's "go" points to the greater Departure, Christ who "emptied himself" (Philippians 2:7) and left his Father's house; Moses' "stop" points to the One who said from the cross, "It is finished" (John 19:30). We do not impose this on the conversation. But in our hearts we know: beginning and end meet in Him.

And we must be honest about the difference: our brothers do not confess the Trinity, nor Jesus as Messiah. That is precisely what makes this dialogue precious — we can name the difference and still bow to learn. Learn what? Learn their seriousness with the text: a portion every week, chanted word by word, the whole Torah traversed in a year. Learn their rhythm of keeping the Sabbath as a sanctuary in time. Here is the scalpel: Christians who claim the fulfillment so often read with less devotion than our brothers. One who cannot keep the Sabbath has no standing to smile at his brother's "blindness."

A Sabbath practice

1. Today, deliberately stop one thing you "must" do — and discover that stopping is also faith. 2. Bless one Jewish household — someone you know, or an unnamed family chanting *Lech-Lecha* in synagogue tonight. 3. Meditate: what is the "go" of my life? What is the "stop"? Place both in God's hands. 4. Take as your anchor, "mount up with wings like eagles," and enter tonight's rest.

Prayer

Lord, we thank you for our elder brothers in the Old Testament. Their devotion in chanting your word rebukes our casualness; their fidelity in keeping the Sabbath exposes our hurry. Bless every household reading *Lech-Lecha* in synagogue tonight. Teach us Abraham's "go" and Moses' "stop"; teach us to mount up with wings like eagles as we wait for you. And reveal to them, in your own time, the One whom David called Lord. Amen.
