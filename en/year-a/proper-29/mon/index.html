What Lectio Is

Benedict of Nursia set fixed hours in his Rule for the monks to read Scripture slowly (Rule of St. Benedict, ch. 48). They did not race. They let the word come in. Today, read all four passages twice. The first time, watch. The second time, listen for the line that stops you.

Four Clocks, and a Fifth

These readings keep four clocks. Ezekiel sits by the river Chebar in Babylon, about 585 BC. A runner has just come in with the news: "The city has been struck down" (Ezek 33:21). No one can date Psalm 100. Pilgrims sang it walking up to the temple gates. Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives in the last week of his life. In two days he will be handed over (Matt 26:2). Paul writes to Ephesus with a chain on his wrist (Eph 6:20).

The fifth clock is ours. November 2026. The church year has one Sunday left, and on it we crown Christ King. A week later Advent comes, and with it the Year of Mark. The year ends by lifting its eyes to a throne.

Start with the Gospel

The Gospel holds the center of the week. Matthew sets this scene at the end of Jesus' last long teaching. Three stories come before it, and each asks: How will you live while the master is away? This one answers: What will he look for when he comes?

The Son of Man takes his throne. The nations gather. He divides them. That is not the surprise. The surprise is that both sides ask the same thing: "Lord, when did we see you?" (25:37, 44). Carry that question all week.

Echoes

Sheep wander through every reading. In Ezekiel, God goes out to hunt for scattered sheep. In the psalm, we are "the sheep of his pasture." In the Gospel, the King sorts sheep from goats. Paul names no sheep, but he looks at the same scene from above: God "gave him as head over all things to the church" (Eph 1:22). The shepherd is the king. The king is the shepherd.

One verb ties three readings. In the Greek of Ezekiel, God says he will gather his sheep (34:13, synaxō). In Matthew the nations are gathered before the throne (25:32). And the righteous, Jesus says, gathered the stranger in (25:35, synēgagete), where English Bibles read "welcomed." God gathers his flock. His flock gathers in the homeless.

Looking Up

Every reading points up to the same Lord. Ezekiel hears "the Lord GOD" say he will search. The psalm says the LORD made us. Paul prays to "the Father of glory," who seated Christ at his right hand. And the King calls the righteous "blessed by my Father." The one on the throne is no distant judge. He went looking first. He set the table before we came.

Looking Down

Neither side saw the King. One side served and forgot. The other side passed by and never knew. The darkness here is not loud. It is blindness. We look at people all day and do not see them.

Today's Practice

Give it twenty minutes, before you leave the house or after the children are asleep. Read the four passages aloud, twice. Sit still for five minutes and let the line that stopped you settle. Write it down. Carry it to work, to the stove, to the hospital. Tonight, look back. Where today, and in whom, might I have met him and not known? Write down one name or one picture. That is enough.

Closing Prayer

Lord, the year runs out,
and you lift our eyes to your throne.
The people there ask you,
When did we see you?
I ask it too.
Open my eyes this week.
On the roads I already walk,
in faces I know too well to see,
let me find you.
Amen.
