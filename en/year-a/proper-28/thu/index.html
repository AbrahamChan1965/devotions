What Gospel Contemplation Is

Ignatius of Loyola, in the Spiritual Exercises, told the one who prays to begin by "seeing the place" (Exx 47). See the ground the text gives. Hear the words it records. Step inside. Add no one. Invent nothing.

Two Places

The first place is the Mount of Olives. Matthew says Jesus sat there, facing the Temple (Matt 24:3). It is Tuesday of the last week. Passover comes in two days. The teller knows he will soon be handed over.

The second place is Matthew's church. Most scholars think he wrote after the Temple fell in AD 70. His readers heard "after a long time the lord of those servants came" and knew where they stood. Inside the long time. So do we.

The Pattern Breaks

The master comes home to settle accounts. The first two servants report and hear the same words. "Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a few things, I will set you over many things. Enter into the joy of your lord" (Matt 25:21). Matthew writes the two scenes almost alike, like the two lines of a couplet.

Then the third servant speaks, and the couplet breaks. "Lord, I knew you that you are a hard man, reaping where you did not sow, and gathering where you did not scatter. I was afraid, and went away and hid your talent in the earth" (25:24-25).

He wasted nothing. The Talmud records the rabbi Shmuel: money is safely kept only in the ground (Bava Metzia 42a). By the law of his day, he did right.

Listen to his order. "I knew you." "I was afraid." "I hid." The hiding grew from the fear. The fear grew from the master he imagined. When the master answers, he hands the man back his own words (25:26). The servant had always lived before a master of his own drawing.

The master calls him "slothful." The Greek, oknēre, means one who hangs back, who shrinks. Barak hung back too. This man did not sleep. He drew in.

Two Pairs of Eyes

Yesterday's psalm watched the master's hand and waited for mercy. This servant also watched his master's hand. He saw a hand that reaps and grabs. One master. Two pairs of eyes. Two different hands.

In the end he is thrown into "the outer darkness." Paul tells believers, "You, brothers, aren't in darkness" (1 Thess 5:4). The master does not push him into the dark. He has lived there all along. Now it is only named.

The story does not end in money. It ends in joy, chara. From the first day God made us, and on the day he saved us, he meant to bring us into his own gladness. The one who told this story went out two days later to be handed over. He went to open that door.

Discerning the False Reason

Ignatius described what the enemy does to those who are turning toward God:

«propio es del mal espíritu morder, tristar y poner impedimentos, inquietando con falsas razones, para que no pase adelante» (Exx 315)
It is the way of the evil spirit to bite, to sadden, to put up obstacles, to unsettle with false reasons, so that the soul does not go forward.

"You are a hard man" is such a reason. It sounds like knowledge. It is a lie. It does not send a man forward. It sends him down, digging. Ignatius also said:

«En tiempo de desolación nunca hacer mudanza, sino estar firme y constante en los propósitos y determinación en que estaba el día antecedente a la tal desolación» (Exx 318)
In a time of desolation, make no change. Stand firm and steady in the purpose and decision you held on the day before the desolation came.

When fear tells you to bury, do not turn in that hour. Go back to the master you saw when your mind was clear. The one who said "Well done." The one who said "Enter." The one who gave himself away two days later.

Today's Practice

Give it twenty minutes. Read Matthew 25:14-30 once. Then stop at 25:24-25 and read it slowly three times. First, hear his voice. Second, sort what the text says of the master from what the servant made up. Third, recall something like it you have said to God, and write one sentence. Beside it write the master's word, "Enter." Sit a while and let the two sentences face each other.

Closing Prayer

Lord, you handed me your house.
You set no terms.
You said you would come back.
I called you hard.
I was afraid.
I buried what was yours
and named it prudence.
Uncover the false reason in me.
Let me see you as you are.
You say, "Well done." You say, "Enter."
Do not let me shrink back.
Do not let me live in the dark.
Raise me up at once
into your joy. Amen.
