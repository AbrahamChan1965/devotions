What Instructio Is

On Friday we read the letter. The apostle turns the gospel story into teaching, so we know where we stand. The questions of the week go to Paul today.

A City on the Road

Thessalonica was the capital of Roman Macedonia. It sat on the Via Egnatia, the great road that ran from the Adriatic toward the east, and it kept a busy harbor. The city honored the emperor. The peace he brought was its pride. Paul preached there a short while and was driven out (Acts 17:1-10). He went on to Corinth, lodged with Priscilla and Aquila, and made tents to pay his way (Acts 18:1-3). He wrote back that he had worked "night and day" among them (1 Thess 2:9). Most scholars date the letter near AD 50. It may be the oldest writing in the New Testament, some twenty years after the resurrection.

The young church had a question. When will the day of the Lord come? The letter left Corinth almost two thousand years ago and reached our hands. We are still asking.

They, You, We

Paul gives no date. "Concerning the times and the seasons, brothers, you have no need that anything be written to you" (5:1). Then he speaks of others. "When they are saying, 'Peace and safety,' then sudden destruction will come on them" (5:3). Some scholars hear in "peace and safety" an echo of Rome's own slogans. The world's sense of safety does not hold back that day.

Then he turns to "you." "But you, brothers, aren't in darkness … You are all children of light, and children of the day" (5:4-5). The mood is plain statement. Not "you should become." You are.

Only then comes "we." "So then let's not sleep, as the rest do, but let's watch and be sober" (5:6). Who you are comes first. What you do comes second. No one earns the name "child of the day" by staying awake. You stay awake because the day has already come for you.

Sleep and Drink in the Night

"Those who sleep, sleep in the night; and those who are drunk are drunk in the night" (5:7). The dark seldom uses terror. It prefers numbness. It puts us to sleep. It makes us drunk on "peace and safety," sure nothing will happen. This is the same dark that held the third servant, with another face. One man is too afraid to move. Another is too comfortable to bother.

Not Appointed to Wrath

The deepest line is here. "For God didn't appoint us to wrath, but to the obtaining of salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us, that, whether we wake or sleep, we should live together with him" (5:9-10).

"Wrath" is orgē. The old prophets called the day of the Lord a day of wrath (see Zeph 1:15). Paul says someone has borne it. "Who died for us." The Greek marks it as done, once. It need not be repeated. It cannot be.

The third servant feared his master's anger. Paul says the master took the anger on himself. "Sleep" here means the believers who have died. Christ's death covers the waking and the sleeping. The living and the resting live with him, and wait together for his coming.

Put It On

So Paul says, "putting on the breastplate of faith and love, and, for a helmet, the hope of salvation" (5:8). He takes the armor from Isaiah 59:17, where God himself wears it. To keep watch is not to stare into the dark with frightened eyes. It is to walk forward dressed in what God gives. The last word is for the many: "Therefore exhort one another, and build each other up" (5:11).

Today's Practice

Give it twenty minutes. Read 1 Thessalonians 5:1-11 and follow the order: they, you, we. Then sit still and ask: Am I held by fear, or put to sleep by ease? Choose one small thing you have set aside because of it, something you can do this week. Not a large thing. Today tell one brother or sister about it, ask them to pray for you, and pray for them.

Closing Prayer

God of the day,
you give me no date.
You give me a name: child of light.
I feared your anger.
I was drunk on peace and safety.
You bore the anger for me.
You died once. You need not die again.
Dress me in faith and love.
Set the hope of salvation on my head,
not because I fear the dark,
but because the day has come.
Waking or sleeping,
I live with you.
Wake me today. Send me forward. Amen.
