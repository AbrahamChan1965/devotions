This week's readings Joshua 24:1-3a, 14-25 Psalm 78:1-7 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18 Matthew 25:1-13 Today: read all four. Listen for the sentence that stops you.

What reading through means

Guigo II, a twelfth-century Carthusian, said reading Scripture is like putting food in the mouth. Meditation chews it. Prayer tastes it (Guigo II, Scala Claustralium). Today, only put the food in the mouth. Do not rush to chew. Read all four passages twice: first hear the story, then hear the sentence that stops you.

Time coordinates

These readings come from three moments. The gathering at Shechem took place when Joshua was old (Josh 23:1-2), at the end of the generation that entered Canaan. The parable of the ten virgins, in Matthew's order, comes after Jesus left the temple and sat on the Mount of Olives (Matt 24:1-3), days from the cross (26:2). First Thessalonians was written about AD 50, while Paul was in Corinth (Acts 18:5).

The Church reads these texts in the last weeks of Ordinary Time, because the year ends by looking toward the Lord's coming. Three weeks from now, Advent begins from the same hope.

Read the Gospel first: all ten slept

The Gospel leads this week. Start here.

The bridegroom delayed, and all ten virgins "slumbered and slept" (Matt 25:5). The wise slept too. The difference is not who stayed awake, but whose lamp could still burn when the cry came.

At midnight the cry goes out: "Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him" (25:6). No one knew the hour. The lamps had to be ready before the cry, because readiness could not be borrowed after it came.

The parable has one command: "Watch therefore" (25:13). The word is γρηγορεῖτε (grēgoreite). Since the wise slept too, watching is not staying awake. Watching means being ready before you sleep. Hold that word. It will walk with us all week.

Shechem: choose in daylight

Joshua gathered the tribes at Shechem and said, "Choose this day whom you will serve" (Josh 24:15). The word for "this day" is הַיּוֹם (hayyôm). The choice sits in daylight, in today, not in some night to come.

The people answered three times, "We will serve the LORD" (24:18, 21, 24). In the middle, Joshua answered with a sentence no one expected (24:19). We will stop there on Tuesday.

The Psalm: tell the children not yet born

Asaph says: tell God's deeds to the next generation, so that children "yet to be born" may know them (Ps 78:6), and set their hope in God and keep his commandments (78:7). Hope and obedience sit in the same verse. People who wait for God wait by keeping covenant.

Thessalonica: do not grieve without hope

Paul wrote to people who had only recently believed: "We do not want you to be uninformed, brothers, about those who are asleep" (1 Thess 4:13). He does not say, do not grieve. He says, do not grieve the way people grieve who have no hope.

Scripture does not say why the letter turns to believers who have died. Most naturally, someone in the church had already died.

The sentence for today

If another sentence stopped you, stay with it. The sentence the Church carries this week is this:

"The Lord himself will descend from heaven" (1 Thess 4:16).

"Himself" is αὐτὸς ὁ κύριος (autos ho kyrios) — the Lord in person. He sends no messenger and leaves no rules in his place. He comes himself. The choice at Shechem, the telling in the Psalm, the lamp at midnight — this week finds its direction in this sentence.

Practice (20 minutes)

  1. Read all four passages slowly. The first time, only hear the story. (6 minutes)
  2. Read them again. Underline the sentence that stops you. (5 minutes)
  3. Copy that sentence out. Read it aloud three times. (3 minutes)
  4. Write down one small thing you can do for the Lord today. Do it today. (3 minutes)
  5. Tonight, light a lamp before bed. Read the sentence. Then put the lamp out with your own hand. (3 minutes)

After the practice, sit quiet. Add nothing more.

The question to carry: How do I live in these days before he comes?

Closing prayer

Lord Jesus, you sat on the Mount of Olives and said the bridegroom would come. You did not say which day. You said you would come yourself. Today you set the choice before me. You carry God's deeds from one generation to the next. You hold hope for those who sleep. Tonight I light the lamp and put it out. You keep this night. In your name, Amen.
