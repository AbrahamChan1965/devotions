What Meditatio Is

Yesterday we took the word in. Today we chew it. Gerard Zerbolt of Zutphen taught readers to walk a text step by step until the mind arrives before God (De spiritualibus ascensionibus). Take one passage. Walk it slowly.

Yesterday's Question

We carried this: While he has not yet come, how do I live?

Shechem answers. Joshua has grown old (Josh 23:1). The people know their leader will leave. He gives no strategy. He gives two things: remember what God has done, and choose today.

Where We Stand in Time

A generation ends here. Shechem holds memory. Abraham built his first altar in Canaan here (Gen 12:6-7). Jacob buried his household gods under the oak near Shechem (Gen 35:4).

The first readings of these weeks run in sequence. Moses dies on Nebo (Deut 34). The ark leads Israel through the Jordan (Josh 3). Now Shechem. The church reads a farewell covenant at the turn of the year to ask one question: when the leader is gone, who still serves?

It Did Not Start with Us

Joshua speaks of God before he speaks of the people. "Long ago, your fathers lived beyond the Euphrates… and they served other gods" (24:2). The chapter's first use of serve (ʿābad) describes idolatry.

Then God speaks: "I took your father Abraham from beyond the River" (24:3). I took: wāʾeqqaḥ. From there God drives every verb. I gave. I sent. I brought out. I handed over. The covenant begins with God's hand, not human zeal.

Choose This Day

Only then does Joshua command: "Fear the LORD and serve him in sincerity and in faithfulness. Put away the gods your fathers served" (24:14).

He opens the door wide: "If it is evil in your eyes to serve the LORD, choose this day whom you will serve" (24:15). Choose: baḥărû. This day: hayyôm. The choice is real. Joshua declares his own first: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

You Are Not Able

The people answer at once: "We also will serve the LORD" (24:18).

Joshua stops them: "You are not able to serve the LORD, for he is a holy God. He is a jealous God; he will not forgive your transgressions or your sins" (24:19). Not able: lōʾ tûkəlû, a word about capacity. Forgive: nāśāʾ, literally lift or bear. Joshua dismantles their confidence.

They answer again: "No, but we will serve the LORD" (24:21). Joshua names them witnesses against themselves. They reply with one word: ʿēdîm, "Witnesses" (24:22). That day Joshua cuts a covenant (24:25).

A Seam Left Open

In Greek, Joshua's name is Iēsous, the same as Jesus. Yet Hebrews says Joshua did not give them rest (Heb 4:8). He cut a covenant. He set up a stone. He could not bear their sins, and he would not return (Josh 24:29).

Elsewhere God proclaims himself the one who forgives iniquity (Exod 34:7), the same verb, nāśāʾ. Isaiah sees one who bore the sin of many (Isa 53:12), the same verb again.

How these meet, we leave unsaid today. Carry the seam into the week.

Practice (20 minutes)

  1. Read Joshua 24:1-3a, 14-25 slowly. Then read the skipped verses, 3b-13. (6 min)
  2. Like Joshua, write three things God has done in your life. Begin each sentence with You. (6 min)
  3. Choose one small act you can do today as your answer: I will serve. (4 min)
  4. Tonight, light a lamp. Read yesterday's verse. Name one work of God aloud. Put out the flame with your own hand. (4 min)

Then sit in silence for a few minutes. Add nothing.

Carry this question: Who keeps the "I will serve" I spoke today?

Closing Prayer

LORD our God, you took Abraham from beyond the River. You led us out of the house of slavery. You gave us gardens we did not plant. You are holy. We say: we will serve. You see that we cannot. Still you cut a covenant with us. You remember what we said today. You bear what we cannot carry. Tonight by lamplight I count your works. In the name of Jesus, amen.
