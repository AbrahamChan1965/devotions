What Gospel Contemplation Is

In the Spiritual Exercises, Ignatius teaches compositio loci: enter the gospel scene with the senses, so the text is seen and heard, not only read (Exx 47). Today we enter only what the parable gives: ten lamps, delay, sleep, a cry, a door. Add nothing.

Yesterday's Question

We carried this: What will I hand down to someone not yet born?

The psalm says God's works can be handed down (Ps 78:4-6). Today the parable names something that cannot be handed over. The wise answer: "There will not be enough for us and for you" (Matt 25:9). Words pass on. Readiness cannot be borrowed. Both are true. Hold both this week.

Where We Stand in Time

Jesus leaves the temple and sits on the Mount of Olives. His disciples come to him privately (Matt 24:1-3). In Matthew's sequence, Passover is two days away (26:2). Jesus tells this parable to his own, days before the cross.

The church reads it at the year's end to hear that cry before the year closes.

The Night

See the lamps first. Ten people. Ten lamps. All go out to meet the bridegroom (25:1). Everyone holds a lamp. The difference hides in the vessels: the wise "took flasks of oil with their lamps" (25:4).

Watch the time. The bridegroom delays (chronizontos). "They all became drowsy and slept" (25:5). All: pasai. The wise slept too. The parable does not rebuke sleep.

Pause. Look at your hand. The lamp is in it.

The Cry

"At midnight there was a cry: 'Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!'" (25:6). Cry: kraugē. The verb, gegonen, is perfect: the cry has sounded and will not be recalled.

Hear it. All rise and trim their lamps (25:7). Only now does the difference show: "Our lamps are going out" (25:8). Sbennyntai, present tense, going out now. The flame shrinks in the hand. The wise answer briefly: there will surely not be enough (ou mē arkesē). This is not stinginess. It is the parable's boundary: readiness cannot be borrowed.

The Door

While they go to buy oil, the bridegroom comes. "Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding feast, and the door was shut" (25:10).

Hear the door close. Then hear the voices outside: "Lord, lord, open to us." From inside: "Truly, I do not know you" (25:11-12).

The parable never explains the oil. It stops here. The heaviest line is not "You had no oil." It is "I do not know you."

One command closes it: "Watch, therefore" (grēgoreite, 25:13). Present tense: keep watching. Since the wise also slept, watching means being ready before sleep comes.

The Same Mountain, Days Later

Matthew goes on. Days later, on the same mountain, at Gethsemane below it, Jesus says, "Remain here, and watch with me" (26:38). The same word. He returns and finds them sleeping (26:40). Three times.

The one who said "Watch" kept watch alone that night. Today we only see it. Tomorrow we walk further.

Discernment

After the parable, notice the movements of your heart.

Ignatius names consolation: "cuando en el alma se causa alguna moción interior, con la cual viene la ánima a inflamarse en amor de su Criador y Señor", an interior movement that sets the soul aflame with love for its Creator and Lord (Exx 316).

He names desolation: "oscuridad del alma, turbación en ella, moción a las cosas bajas y terrenas, inquietud de varias agitaciones y tentaciones", darkness of soul, turmoil, a pull toward low and earthly things, restlessness from many agitations and temptations (Exx 317).

Exx 318: "En tiempo de desolación nunca hacer mudanza, sino estar firme y constante en los propósitos y determinación en que estaba el día antecedente a la tal desolación." In desolation, never make a change. Stand firm in the resolution you held the day before the desolation came.

What you chose by day, do not undo at midnight. On Tuesday, at Shechem, we chose by daylight.

Practice (25 minutes)

  1. Read Matthew 25:1-13 aloud, slowly. (4 min)
  2. Close your eyes. Enter three places in turn: the lamps, the cry, the door. Stay two minutes in each. (6 min)
  3. Immersion: Hold your lamp. Stand at the door. Listen for either "I know you" or "I do not know you." Stay silent. Do not rush to answer. (5 min)
  4. Use Exx 316–318 to discern whether that movement was consolation or desolation. Write one word. (5 min)
  5. Tonight, light a lamp. Read this week's verse. Say quietly: What I chose by day, I keep at midnight. Put out the flame with your own hand. (5 min)

Then sit in silence for a few minutes. Add nothing.

Carry this question: Does he know me?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, you sat on the Mount of Olives and said the bridegroom would come. You did not name the night. We all fell asleep; you did not rebuke our sleep. When the cry sounds, raise us with our lamps still lit. In Gethsemane you asked your friends to watch with you. They slept. You watched alone. You watched for us. From inside the door you speak my name. In your name, amen.
