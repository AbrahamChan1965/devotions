In the Synagogue This Week Torah: Parashat Chayei Sarah (Genesis 23:1–25:18) Prophets: 1 Kings 1:1-31 ← today's focus

What Sabbath Reading Is

Today synagogues around the world read the Torah and the Prophets. We do not cross over to read for them. We sit across from them, two tables facing each other.

We listen from within the atonement Christ has finished. Our security rests there, not in winning arguments. So we can listen without disputing, and without taking over.

Yesterday's Question

We carried this: What sentence can I use this week to comfort someone?

The synagogue table has its own sentence. When Jews visit mourners, they traditionally say: "May the Omnipresent (HaMakom) comfort you among all the mourners of Zion and Jerusalem." Both tables hold words for those who weep. Today we listen to theirs first.

Where We Stand in Time

The Torah portion stands in the age of the patriarchs, at Hebron. The prophetic portion stands at the dawn of the kingdom, in David's last days. Today is the 27th of Cheshvan. The synagogue also blesses the coming new month.

This same week, the church reads of those who sleep and of the Lord's coming.

The Synagogue Table: A Grave

This portion is called "The Life of Sarah," yet it opens with her death. "Abraham went in to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her" (Gen 23:2). The week's first act is tears.

Then Abraham rises and speaks to the Hittites: "I am a sojourner and foreigner among you; give me property among you for a burying place, that I may bury my dead" (23:4). Gēr-wətôšāb: a sojourner asks for a possession (ʾăḥuzzâ). It is the same word God used when he promised Abraham an "everlasting possession" (17:8).

In the promised land, his first possession is a grave. He pays four hundred shekels at full merchant weight (23:16). He does not seize it. He buys it openly. Later Isaac and Ishmael bury him there together (25:9).

The Synagogue Table: An Old King

The prophetic portion opens: "King David was old and advanced in years" (1 Kgs 1:1). The words match Abraham's old age (Gen 24:1). The patriarch has grown old. The king has grown old. A legacy and a throne must pass to the next generation.

Adonijah exalts himself: "I will be king" (1 Kgs 1:5). David swears by "the LORD who has redeemed my soul out of every adversity" and sets Solomon in his place (1:29-30). Redeemed: pādâ.

Bathsheba bows to the ground: "May my lord King David live forever!" (1:31). She speaks it to a dying king.

How the Synagogue Waits

This Sabbath the synagogue blesses the new month. Its prayer remembers how God redeemed the ancestors from slavery, and asks God to redeem soon and gather the scattered.

They wait by remembering. They wait by counting months. They wait Sabbath by Sabbath. This is Wednesday's psalm: set your hope in God and keep his commandments (Ps 78:7).

The Church Table

This week the church reads, "The Lord himself will descend from heaven" (1 Thess 4:16), and the midnight cry, "Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!" (Matt 25:6).

We also weep for those who sleep, but not as those who have no hope (1 Thess 4:13).

Between the Two Tables

Both tables wait in grief. Both believe God redeems. Both keep covenant while they wait.

They part at one point: Who is the Redeemer, and has he come? The synagogue says, we still wait for him to come. The church says, he has come. He died, he rose, and he himself will come again.

The words stop there. No resolution. No closing.

Practice (20 minutes)

  1. Read Genesis 23:1-20 and 1 Kings 1:28-31 slowly. (6 min)
  2. Stay with Genesis 23:4. Read it aloud: I am a sojourner among you. (3 min)
  3. The standing practice: "Today I heard a sentence I cannot claim as mine: ____." Write it. Do not explain. Add nothing. (5 min)
  4. Tonight, light a lamp. Read this week's verse. Only sit. Add nothing. Put out the flame with your own hand. (6 min)

Then sit in silence for a few minutes. Add nothing.

Carry this question: What I hold in my hands—is it an inheritance, or a plot of ground for waiting?

Closing Prayer

The One to whom we pray created heaven and earth. He chose Abraham. He chose Israel. Israel does not possess him; he claims Israel as his own. So we call him the Holy One of Israel.

Holy One of Israel, Abraham wept before you for Sarah, and you heard. A sojourner bought a grave; you remember your promise. An old king swore by your name and said you redeemed him from every adversity. Today the synagogue and the church both wait; you hear the prayers of both tables. In the name of Jesus, amen.
