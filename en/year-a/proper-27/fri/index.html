What Conformatio Is

Paul longs to be "conformed to his death" (Phil 3:10). Symmorphizomenos: shaped into his form. That death does not end in death. It passes through the rest of the tomb, into resurrection, toward the Spirit and the coming. Today all four stand in view.

Yesterday's Question

We carried this: Does he know me?

Paul opens this letter: "We know, brothers loved by God, that he has chosen you" (1 Thess 1:4). Know: eidotes. In this letter, to be known is to be loved and chosen. Today we see how far that knowing reaches. It holds even those who sleep.

Where We Stand in Time

Driven out of Thessalonica (Acts 17:10), Paul sent Timothy back from Athens (1 Thess 3:1-2). Timothy returned to Corinth with news (3:6; Acts 18:5). Paul wrote this letter around AD 50. Why it turns to believers who have died, the text does not say. The plainest reading: someone in the young church had died.

On All Saints' Day, November 1, the church remembered those who sleep. Now, at the year's end, it reads this passage and waits for Advent.

Passion: He Died

Paul writes: "We believe that Jesus died and rose again" (4:14). For Jesus he writes apethanen, died. For believers he writes slept.

Yesterday, in Gethsemane, the disciples slept and Jesus watched alone. After that night he truly died, once, completely. "Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many" (Heb 9:28). Tuesday's seam closes here. At Shechem the holy God would not bear their sins. In his Son, he bore them himself. That bear comes from Isaiah 53:12.

Holy Saturday: Those Who Sleep

He took real death, so the death of those who trust him becomes sleep. Koimēthentas: those fallen asleep. The tomb becomes rest that waits for morning.

Paul does not say, "Do not grieve." He says, do not grieve as those who have no hope (4:13). Keep the tears. Lay down despair.

Resurrection: God Will Bring

"God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep through Jesus" (4:14). God drives the verb. Axei: he will bring. On Wednesday we saw that God keeps watch. Today we see that he does not let go even of those who sleep.

Yesterday the ten virgins rose (ēgerthēsan), Matthew's resurrection word. Today Paul says, "The dead in Christ will rise first" (4:16).

Spirit and Coming: The Lord Himself

"The Lord himself will descend from heaven with a cry of command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God" (4:16). Monday's verse returns.

Israel saw this before. At Sinai, where the covenant was made, there were thick cloud and a trumpet blast. The LORD came down. Moses "brought the people out of the camp to meet God" (Exod 19:16-20). The covenant began with God descending. It will be completed when God descends.

Meet is apantēsis (4:17), the same word as the midnight cry: "Come out to meet him!" (Matt 25:6). Then the shortest sentence: "So we will always be with the Lord." Syn kyriō, with the Lord.

In the waiting, God "gives his Holy Spirit to you" (1 Thess 4:8). The Spirit is the pledge of that knowing. The Spirit keeps the flame alive through the wait.

Gathering the Week

Monday, we heard: the Lord himself. Tuesday: you are not able. Wednesday: God keeps watch. Thursday: at the door, we saw him watching alone. Today it all gathers.

The deepest root of watching is not that I stay awake. It is that he died and rose. Even asleep, I am in him. "Therefore encourage one another with these words" (4:18).

Practice (20 minutes)

  1. Read 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18 slowly. Circle every with. (4 min)
  2. Sit with this: he died, so I only sleep. (4 min)
  3. Think of someone grieving. Choose one sentence from 4:13-18. Write it to them. Send it today. (6 min)

Do only this one small thing. Do not choose a large one. Large gestures often aim to finish something, not to love.

  1. Tonight, light a lamp. Read this week's verse. Say quietly: Even asleep, I am in him. Put out the flame with your own hand. (6 min)

Then sit in silence for a few minutes. Add nothing.

A question to ponder: In my grief, which part is tears, and which part is hopelessness?

Carry this question: What sentence can I use this week to comfort someone?

Closing Prayer

Father, your Son kept watch in the garden; we slept. He died, once and for all, and bore what we could not carry. You raised him from the dead. You will bring those who sleep with him. You give your Spirit, and the lamp stays lit through the wait. The Lord himself will descend; we will always be with the Lord. You put words of comfort in our mouths. In the name of Jesus, amen.
