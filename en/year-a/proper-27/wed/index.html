What Oratio Is

Guigo II wrote: reading puts food in the mouth, meditation chews, prayer tastes (Scala Claustralium). Today we stop analyzing. We pray the psalm in three movements: lean in, tell, hope.

Today we read only the psalm and Joshua. The New Testament waits for Thursday and Friday.

Yesterday's Question

We carried this: Who keeps the "I will serve" I spoke today?

At Shechem the people promised three times. Joshua answered, "You are not able." The psalm points elsewhere. A generation's vow does not hold the covenant. God "established a testimony in Jacob" (Ps 78:5), and God hands it down, generation to generation.

Where We Stand in Time

Asaph led song in the sanctuary in David's day (1 Chr 25:1-2). The psalm runs all the way to God choosing David to shepherd his people (Ps 78:70-72). It likely took shape in David's time or soon after.

The poet stands at the dawn of the kingdom and looks back: Egypt, the wilderness, Canaan. He tells the story of the generations after Joshua. In the week of Shechem, the church sings this psalm to let it answer Shechem: how does a covenant outlive the next generation?

First Prayer: Lean In

"Give ear, O my people, to my teaching; incline your ears to the words of my mouth" (78:1).

Incline: haṭṭû. At Shechem Joshua said, "Incline your heart to the LORD" (Josh 24:23). The same verb, the same form. Shechem bends the heart. The psalm first bends the ear.

Prayer begins here. Draw near. Turn your head toward the one who speaks.

Second Prayer: Hide Nothing

"We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD" (78:4).

The poet says, "I will open my mouth in a parable" (78:2), then tells history. Matthew later quotes this verse of Jesus' parables (Matt 13:35). We enter the parable on Thursday. Today we only mark the thread.

Then the turn: "He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel" (78:5). God drives the verb. Testimony: ʿēdût. It shares a root with the people's one-word answer at Shechem: ʿēdîm, "Witnesses." The people's witness wavers. God's testimony stands.

The telling crosses four generations. Fathers tell us. We tell our children. Our children tell "children yet unborn" (78:6). Here prayer becomes intercession for someone not yet born.

Third Prayer: Hope and Keep Watch

Why tell? "So that they should set their hope in God and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments" (78:7).

Keep: nāṣar, the verb of guarding and watching. Watchmen are nōṣərîm (Jer 31:6). In Hebrew, keeping covenant and keeping watch are one act.

But God keeps watch first. Of his vineyard he says, "I, the LORD, am its keeper… I guard it night and day, so that no one may harm it" (Isa 27:3), the same word. Here yesterday's question finds its answer. We sleep. The Keeper of Israel neither slumbers nor sleeps (Ps 121:4).

The Reading Stops at Verse Seven

The reading ends at verse 7. Verse 8 begins: "that they should not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation." Most of the seventy-two verses count failures.

The church reads only seven verses. It does not pretend the failures did not happen. It lets hope speak first. Pray from that honest place: our weakness cannot be handed down; God's works can.

Practice (20 minutes)

  1. Read Psalm 78:1-7 slowly, then Joshua 24:23. (4 min)
  2. Read verse 1. Tilt your head slightly. Listen in silence for two minutes. (3 min)
  3. Write one work of God in your life you have never told anyone. (5 min)
  4. Write the name of someone in the next generation. Pray Psalm 78:7 over that name. (5 min)
  5. Tonight, light a lamp. Read this week's verse. Speak that name and pray one sentence for them. Put out the flame with your own hand. (3 min)

Then sit in silence for a few minutes. Add nothing.

Carry this question: What will I hand down to someone not yet born?

Closing Prayer

God who keeps Israel, you established a testimony in Jacob, you appointed a law in Israel. You open the father's mouth, you bend the child's ear. You guard your vineyard night and day; you do not slumber, you do not sleep. We sleep. You do not. I place a child's name in your hands: (speak the name). Let them set their hope in you. Let them remember your works. In the name of Jesus, amen.
