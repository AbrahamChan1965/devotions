Today: read all four, and find one line that stops you

What Is Lectio?

In chapter 48 of his Rule, Benedict of Nursia set fixed hours each day for his monks to read Scripture slowly (lectio divina). No hurry. Let the words in first. Today, read all four passages from start to finish, twice, out loud. The first time, watch the pictures. The second time, listen for the line that stops you.

Where We Are in Time

This week's readings stand at four moments.

Isaiah 40 speaks first, to the exiles. Jerusalem fell in 587 BC and its people were taken to Babylon. By about 540 BC they had lived in a foreign land for nearly fifty years.

Psalm 85 was sung after the return. In 538 BC the Persian king Cyrus let them go home. They got the land back. Life stayed hard.

Mark 1 was written in the late 60s or around AD 70. Christians in Rome had just lived through Nero's persecution, and the temple in Jerusalem was under siege or in flames.

Second Peter was written to the second generation. The apostles were dying one by one, and the Lord had not come back.

The fifth moment is ours: the second week of Advent, a little over three weeks before Christmas.

Read the Gospel First: A Voice in the Wilderness

The Gospel is the spine of the week. Mark skips the birth of Jesus. His first words are "The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God" (Mark 1:1). Then comes a voice in the wilderness: "Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight" (Mark 1:3).

Carry this picture through the week: by the Jordan, a man in camel's hair stands knee-deep in the water. A long line waits on the bank. One by one, people from the city wade in.

Hear the Echoes

All four passages talk about a road, and about someone coming.

Isaiah says, "In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD" (Isa 40:3), and "the Lord GOD comes with might" (40:10). The psalm says, "Righteousness will go before him and make his footsteps a way" (Ps 85:13). Mark says John is preparing the Lord's way. Peter says, "The day of the Lord will come like a thief" (2 Pet 3:10), and we are to be "waiting for and hastening" it (3:12).

There is grass, too. Isaiah: "All flesh is grass" (Isa 40:6). Peter: "With the Lord one day is as a thousand years" (2 Pet 3:8). Grass dries up in a day. God's word stands forever.

Look Up: The God Who Comes Himself

Looking upward, all four point to a God who will not just send a memo. He tells his messengers to speak to Jerusalem's heart (Isa 40:2). He carries lambs in his arms like a shepherd (40:11). He "will speak peace to his people" (Ps 85:8). He "is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish" (2 Pet 3:9). And Mark says the one coming after John "will baptize you with the Holy Spirit" (Mark 1:8).

Look Down: None to Comfort Her

Lamentations says it five times: "She has none to comfort her" (Lam 1:2, 9, 16, 17, 21). Why? Because the "lovers" she had chased all turned on her. The comfort this week runs deep because the pain under it runs deep, and so does the sin.

Today's Practice

Twenty minutes. Read each passage twice, out loud.

Choose one line that stops you. Write it on a card and carry it all day. Your phone's lock screen works too.

Tonight, ask: when did that line come back to find me today?

Closing Prayer

Lord,
this week I am walking into the wilderness.
No crowds there, only wind and water.
Speak to my heart there.
Let me hear that voice:
Prepare the way of the Lord.
In Jesus' name, amen.
